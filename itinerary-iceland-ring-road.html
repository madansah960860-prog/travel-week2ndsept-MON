Iceland South Coast in three days

Reykjavik out and back, one night moved east, and roughly 480 miles of driving. Built for two travelers in a small rental car between May and September.

  • 3 days
  • 2 travelers
  • 480 miles driving
  • $646 in activities and meals
Copy this into the planner Read the Iceland guide

Costs exclude flights, lodging, insurance and the rental car itself.

Book before you fly

  • The rental car, at least eight weeks out
  • Night two lodging near Kirkjubaejarklaustur, first of everything
  • The glacier lagoon boat tour, two weeks out
  • Secret Lagoon entry, same week is usually fine

Check every morning

  • vedur.is for wind and weather warnings
  • road.is for live road status and closures
  • Sunrise and sunset times for your dates
  • The aurora forecast if you are traveling September to March

Pack for this plan

  • A genuinely waterproof shell, not a resistant one
  • Waterproof shoes with grip for wet basalt
  • Swimsuit and quick-dry towel for the lagoon
  • A card with a four-digit PIN for unattended gas pumps

The plan, day by day

Times assume summer daylight. If you are traveling between November and February, cut the last stop of each day and start an hour later.

Day 1

Golden Circle, taken slowly

160 miles · ease in
  • 8:00 AM Breakfast in Reykjavik Cardamom buns and coffee downtown. Do this before collecting the car, not after, so nobody is hungry during the rental paperwork. Walk 10 min · 0.5 miles
  • 9:00 AM Collect the rental car Photograph every existing scratch and the windshield before you leave the lot. Confirm gravel protection is on the contract. Drive 45 min · 30 miles
  • 10:00 AM Thingvellir National Park Park at P1 for the Almannagja walk down the rift between the North American and Eurasian plates. About 90 minutes at a relaxed pace. Parking is paid and card only. Drive 50 min · 37 miles
  • 12:45 PM Geysir geothermal field Strokkur erupts every 6 to 10 minutes. Stand upwind. The visitor center cafeteria is unremarkable but convenient for lunch. Drive 12 min · 6 miles
  • 2:30 PM Gullfoss Upper platform for the full width of the falls, lower path for the spray. The lower path closes when icy, which happens well outside winter. Drive 1 hr 5 min · 45 miles
  • 4:30 PM Secret Lagoon, Fludir A natural geothermal pool with a fraction of the crowd and price of the famous lagoon. Shower before entering; this is not optional and staff will remind you. Drive 40 min · 28 miles
  • 7:00 PM Dinner and overnight near Hella or Selfoss Cheaper than staying in the capital and 40 minutes closer to tomorrow's first stop.
Estimated day cost
$186 2 travelers · entries, lagoon, 2 meals, fuel · excludes lodging and car rental
Day 2

Waterfalls, then black sand

150 miles · longest driving day
  • 8:30 AM Seljalandsfoss The one you can walk behind. You will get soaked, so this is a shell-and-waterproof-shoes stop. Walk five minutes further to Gljufrabui, hidden in a cleft and usually empty. Drive 20 min · 17 miles
  • 10:30 AM Skogafoss Stand at the base for the scale, then climb the 527 steps for the top view. Skip the climb if the wind is above 30 mph; the exposed stair is genuinely unpleasant. Drive 15 min · 8 miles
  • 12:00 PM Solheimasandur plane wreck, optional A 2.5-mile flat walk each way across black gravel with no shade or shelter. Skip it unless the photo matters to you; a shuttle runs if you would rather not walk. Drive 20 min · 12 miles
  • 1:00 PM Lunch and resupply in Vik Last reliable grocery store heading east. Fill the tank here regardless of what the gauge says, and buy tomorrow's breakfast. Drive 10 min · 5 miles
  • 2:30 PM Reynisfjara black sand beach Basalt columns, sea stacks and genuinely dangerous water. Sneaker waves arrive without warning. Stay above the highest wet line and never turn your back on the ocean. Drive 1 hr 50 min · 87 miles
  • 5:30 PM Check in near Kirkjubaejarklaustur A small town with very limited rooms. This is the booking that constrains the whole trip, so secure it before you buy flights. Drive 8 min · 4 miles
  • 8:00 PM Fjadrargljufur canyon at low light A short walk along the rim of a 300-foot mossy canyon. Stay on the marked path; the vegetation here is fragile and sections have been closed for recovery.
Estimated day cost
$142 2 travelers · groceries, 1 restaurant meal, fuel, parking · excludes lodging
Day 3

Glacier lagoon, then the long drive back

170 miles · big finish
  • 8:00 AM Drive east to Jokulsarlon The longest single leg of the trip. Leave on time; the boat tour will not wait and refunds are not given for late arrivals. Drive 1 hr 40 min · 84 miles
  • 10:00 AM Glacier lagoon boat tour Amphibious boats run roughly May to October and zodiacs go further into the lagoon. Book two weeks ahead. Dress one layer warmer than the air temperature suggests. Walk 8 min · 0.4 miles
  • 11:45 AM Diamond Beach Ice calved from the glacier washed up on black sand, directly across the ring road. Fifteen to twenty minutes is enough unless the light is exceptional. Drive 55 min · 40 miles
  • 1:30 PM Lunch at Skaftafell visitor center Simple food, clean restrooms and the last reliable stop before the hike. Fill water bottles here. Walk 5 min · 0.2 miles
  • 2:15 PM Svartifoss hike 3.5 miles round trip with 400 feet of climb, on a well-marked gravel path. The falls drop over hexagonal basalt columns that inspired Reykjavik's cathedral. Drive 2 hr 40 min · 152 miles
  • 7:15 PM Return to Reykjavik Refuel before the road turns inland at Selfoss. Stations become sparse and several are unattended after 8 PM.
Estimated day cost
$318 2 travelers · boat tour, park fees, 2 meals, fuel · excludes lodging

Where this plan usually breaks

Day 2 runs long. If you add the plane wreck walk and linger at Reynisfjara, you will reach Kirkjubaejarklaustur after dark. Drop one.

Wind cancels Day 3. Boat tours are suspended in high wind. Have a plan B: the Skaftafell hike plus a slower drive back works fine.

Jet lag hits Day 1. Overnight flights from the US land at 6 AM. If you go straight from the plane to the Golden Circle you will not enjoy it.

If you have more days

The south coast rewards extra time far more than the full ring road rewards being rushed.

Day 4: Snaefellsnes peninsula

A compact version of everything Iceland does, two hours north of Reykjavik. Kirkjufell, black church at Budir, and far fewer people than the south.

Day 5: Landmannalaugar

Rhyolite mountains in colors that look edited. Requires a 4x4 and an F-road, and is only open roughly late June to early September.

Day 6 and 7: continue east

Instead of returning from Jokulsarlon, keep going to the East Fjords and complete the ring road over a week. This is the version we would choose given ten days.
