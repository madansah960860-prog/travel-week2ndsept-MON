Four habits that make a plan survive contact with the trip

Drawn from the itineraries people actually finish, compared against the ones they abandon on day two.

Leave one slot empty every day

A fully booked day has no room for the restaurant you walk past and want to try, or the two hours you lose to rain. The empty slot is not wasted; it is the part of the trip you will remember.

Put the hardest thing first

Sunrise hikes, timed-entry tickets and long drives belong at the start of a day. Anything scheduled after 4 PM has a much higher chance of being skipped.

Check the arrival day honestly

If you land at 3 PM after a red-eye from a US East Coast airport, that day holds one stop, not four. Wildroot lightens arrival days by default and you can override it.

Book the awkward night first

On most routes there is one night in a small town with three lodging options. Book that one before anything else, then build the days around it.
