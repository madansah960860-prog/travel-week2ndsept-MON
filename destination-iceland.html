Iceland, planned around the weather instead of against it

Five to ten days, one rental car, and a schedule loose enough to survive a closed mountain road. Here is what it costs, when to go, and where US travelers most often get the pacing wrong.

  • 5 to 10 days
  • Rental car essential
  • About $210 per person per day
See the 3-day plan Plan your own

Keflavik is a 5 to 7 hour flight from most US East Coast hubs.

Overview

Iceland is the rare destination where the drive is the trip. Nearly everything worth seeing sits within a mile of Route 1, the ring road that circles the island, and the country is small enough that you can stand in a lava field at breakfast and a glacier lagoon by mid-afternoon.

What surprises most first-time visitors is not the scenery. It is the cost and the wind. A sit-down dinner for two runs higher than in most American cities, and a moderate gale can shut a mountain pass with no notice at all. Both are manageable if you plan for them, and both wreck trips that assume otherwise.

For a first visit from the United States, we recommend the south coast rather than the full ring road. Five days on the south coast is a good trip. Five days trying to circle the island is 1,300 miles of driving and very little standing still. The full ring road deserves ten days, minimum.

English is spoken essentially everywhere. Card payment works everywhere, including gas pumps and public restrooms, and you can complete a two-week trip without ever handling cash. Bring a card with no foreign transaction fee and a four-digit PIN, because some unattended pumps require one.

Fast facts

Currency
Icelandic króna. Cards accepted nearly everywhere.
Language
Icelandic. English is widely and fluently spoken.
Entry for US citizens
No visa for short tourist stays. Check current requirements with the US Department of State before booking.
Driving
US license accepted for short visits. Right-hand side, same as home.
Tipping
Not expected. Service is included in the listed price.
Time zone
GMT year round. Four to eight hours ahead of the continental US.

Best time to visit

There is no bad month, only different trade-offs between daylight, road access, crowds and price. Pick the trade-off you want.

June to August: the safe choice

Nearly 24 hours of usable light, every road open, and the highland F-roads accessible from late June. Temperatures run 50°F to 60°F. This is also peak price and peak crowding at the famous stops.

Best for: first visits, families, anyone driving the full ring road.

September to October: our pick

Roads are still open, prices drop, the crowds thin noticeably, and the northern lights become possible again from late September. Days shorten to around 11 hours by mid-October.

Best for: photographers, couples, travelers who want the south coast without the tour buses.

November to March: aurora season

Four to six hours of daylight in December, real winter driving, and highland roads closed. The northern lights are at their most likely, and ice caves are only accessible now. Book a guided ice cave tour; do not go alone.

Best for: aurora-focused trips based near Reykjavík or Vík.

April to May: the quiet shoulder

Lengthening days, low prices, and the lowest crowds of the year. Some mountain roads are still closed and the weather is genuinely unsettled. Puffins arrive in late April.

Best for: budget-conscious trips with a flexible schedule.

Where the money actually goes

Lodging and food are the two lines that move the total. Cutting one restaurant dinner per day saves more than any other single change. Grocery stores in Reykjavík and Selfoss are noticeably cheaper than the ones attached to gas stations along the ring road.

Round-trip flights from the US

Typical economy round trip to Keflavík, booked two to four months ahead:

  • Boston or New York: $420 to $700
  • Chicago or Washington DC: $520 to $850
  • Denver or Dallas: $640 to $1,000
  • West Coast: $700 to $1,150, usually with one connection

Fares move constantly. Treat these as planning ranges, not prices.

Getting around

Rent a car. There is no realistic alternative for the south coast, and organized bus tours cost more than a rental once there are two of you.

What to rent

A small two-wheel-drive car handles the ring road and the south coast in every month of the year. You need four-wheel drive only for the highland F-roads, which are open roughly late June to early September and are illegal to drive in a 2WD vehicle regardless of conditions.

Insurance that matters

Take the gravel protection and, if you are traveling between October and April, the sand and ash protection. Both cover damage that standard US credit-card coverage frequently excludes. Read your card's benefit guide before you decline anything at the counter.

Weather and road closures

Two websites decide your day: the Icelandic Met Office for weather warnings and road.is for live road conditions. Check both at breakfast, every day. A wind warning is not a suggestion. Car doors have been torn off by gusts in open parking lots.

Fuel

Stations are self-service and card-operated. Some require a four-digit PIN. Fill up whenever you are below half a tank east of Vík, because the gaps between stations grow quickly.

Where to stay

Reykjavík, nights 1 and final. Base near Laugavegur for walkability. Anything within the 101 postal code is fine.

Selfoss or Hella, night 2. Central for the Golden Circle and the first waterfalls, and cheaper than the capital.

Vík or Kirkjubæjarklaustur, night 3. Very limited inventory. Book first, plan around it second.

Skip: staying at the airport. Keflavík is 45 minutes from Reykjavík and there is nothing there.

Safety notes

Iceland has very low crime and genuinely dangerous nature. The risks are almost entirely environmental.

Sneaker waves

At Reynisfjara and other black sand beaches, waves arrive without warning and pull people out. Never turn your back on the water, and stay well above the highest wet line on the sand.

Wind

Open the car door into the wind, holding it firmly. Gusts routinely exceed 50 mph and door damage is not covered by standard rental insurance.

Glaciers and ice caves

Never walk on a glacier without a guide and crampons. Ice caves are only safe in winter and only with a licensed operator.

River crossings

Do not ford rivers on F-roads unless you are experienced and in a properly equipped vehicle. Rental insurance excludes water damage entirely.

Hot springs

Geothermal water at natural pools can exceed 200°F. Test with a hand before entering, and stay on marked paths in geothermal fields.

If something goes wrong

The emergency number is 112, and there is a free 112 Iceland app that transmits your location. Register your itinerary with SafeTravel.is before any hiking.

Travel insurance with medical evacuation is worth carrying here.
