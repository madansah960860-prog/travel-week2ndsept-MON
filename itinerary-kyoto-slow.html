Three slow days in Kyoto

Two districts a day, temple gates at opening, and roughly seven miles of walking daily. Built for two travelers with no rental car and one reserved dinner.

  • 3 days
  • 2 travelers
  • Rail and walking
  • $379 in activities and meals
Copy this into the planner Read the Kyoto guide

Costs exclude flights, lodging and travel insurance.

Reserve before you fly

  • Lodging, five to six months ahead for late November
  • One special dinner, three to four weeks ahead
  • A tea ceremony if you want one, two weeks ahead
  • Nothing else. Temples do not take reservations.

Buy on arrival

  • An IC transit card at the airport station
  • A pocket wifi device or an eSIM data plan
  • Coin-locker change, though most now take IC cards
  • An umbrella from any convenience store if it rains

Pack for this plan

  • Shoes you can slip off easily, and socks without holes
  • A small towel; many restrooms have no dryer
  • A day bag small enough for crowded trains
  • Cash, because small restaurants and shrines are cash only

The plan, day by day

Start times matter more here than anywhere else in our library. A temple at 7 AM and the same temple at 11 AM are different experiences.

Day 1

Southern Higashiyama

6.8 miles on foot · very early start
  • 6:15 AM Train to Inari station The JR Nara line from Kyoto Station takes five minutes. Trains run from before 5 AM. Train 12 min · 3 miles
  • 6:30 AM Fushimi Inari torii trail Open 24 hours and free. Climb at least as far as the Yotsutsuji junction, about 30 minutes up, for the city view. Above that the crowds vanish entirely. Train 15 min · 3.5 miles
  • 9:15 AM Breakfast at Nishiki Market Tamagoyaki, soy milk doughnuts, tsukemono. Eat at the stall you bought from rather than walking; it is the local norm and stall owners appreciate it. Bus 18 min · 2 miles
  • 11:15 AM Kiyomizu-dera The wooden stage over the hillside, with no nails in the structure. This is the busiest site in Kyoto by noon, so move through steadily and enjoy the view from the far side. Walk 15 min · 0.7 miles
  • 1:00 PM Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lanes Preserved stone streets sloping down toward Yasaka Shrine. Lunch anywhere along here. The steps are genuinely slippery in rain. Walk 20 min · 0.9 miles
  • 3:30 PM Kodai-ji and the bamboo path A quieter temple with a small bamboo grove and a hillside garden. A useful antidote to the crowds an hour earlier. Walk 12 min · 0.6 miles
  • 5:30 PM Gion at dusk Walk Hanamikoji and the Shirakawa canal. Photography is prohibited on the private lanes and enforced with fines. Watch, do not chase.
Estimated day cost
$118 2 travelers · temple entries, transit, 3 meals · excludes lodging
Day 2

Arashiyama and the northwest

7.2 miles on foot · most transit
  • 7:00 AM Train to Saga-Arashiyama The JR Sagano line from Kyoto Station. Leaving this early is the entire trick to Arashiyama. Train 17 min · 7 miles
  • 7:30 AM Arashiyama bamboo grove Enter from the north gate near Okochi Sanso. By 10 AM the main path is shoulder to shoulder; at this hour you may have it to yourself. Walk 6 min · 0.3 miles
  • 9:00 AM Okochi Sanso villa garden The former home of a silent-film actor, with a hillside loop of about 40 minutes. Entry includes matcha and a sweet at the teahouse at the end. Walk 12 min · 0.6 miles
  • 10:45 AM Tenryu-ji pond garden A 14th-century garden designed to borrow the mountains behind it. Garden and buildings are separate tickets; the garden is the one that matters. Walk 15 min · 0.7 miles
  • 12:30 PM Lunch beside the Katsura river Set lunches here run a third of what the same kitchens charge at dinner. Train 25 min · 8 miles
  • 2:30 PM Kinkaku-ji, the golden pavilion One fixed one-way loop, about 30 minutes end to end. Go for the reflection across the pond, then move on without guilt. Bus 15 min · 2 miles
  • 4:00 PM Ryoan-ji rock garden Fifteen stones in raked gravel, arranged so you can never see all fifteen at once. Sit on the veranda for twenty minutes; that is the whole point.
Estimated day cost
$164 2 travelers · 4 entries, transit, 3 meals · excludes lodging
Day 3

The eastern hills, slowly

5.4 miles on foot · reserved dinner
  • 8:30 AM Nanzen-ji and the aqueduct A Meiji-era brick aqueduct running straight through a Zen temple complex. The grounds are free; the Hojo garden is a small separate fee and worth it. Walk 8 min · 0.4 miles
  • 10:00 AM Philosopher's Path A canal-side walk of about 1.2 miles lined with cherry trees, with small temples on either side. Twenty minutes if you rush, ninety if you do it properly. Walk 30 min · 1.2 miles
  • 11:45 AM Ginkaku-ji, the silver pavilion Never actually silvered. The moss garden and the raked sand cone are the reason to come, and it is consistently quieter than its golden counterpart. Bus 22 min · 3 miles
  • 1:30 PM Lunch near Okazaki Quiet museum district with unhurried cafes. A good place to sit down after two hard-walking days. Walk 18 min · 0.9 miles
  • 3:00 PM Nijo Castle Nightingale floors built to squeak underfoot as a security measure. The audio guide is included with entry and genuinely good. Subway 12 min · 2 miles
  • 6:30 PM Reserved dinner on Pontocho Alley A lantern-lit lane one street back from the Kamo river. Most rooms seat eight to twelve people, which is why reserving weeks ahead matters.
Estimated day cost
$97 2 travelers · entries, transit, lunch · excludes the reserved dinner and lodging

Where this plan usually slips

Day 1 starts too late. A 6:30 AM Fushimi Inari becomes an 8:30 AM Fushimi Inari and the whole day loses its shape.

Jet lag on Day 1. If you land the previous evening from the US, expect to wake at 4 AM. That is convenient here, so use it.

Day 2 gets stretched. Adding the monkey park or a boat ride pushes Kinkaku-ji past closing. Pick one.

If you have more days

Kyoto supports a week without repeating itself, and two nearby cities make excellent day trips.

Day 4: Nara

Forty-five minutes by train. Todai-ji holds one of the largest bronze Buddhas in the world, and the deer in the park are wild animals, not pets.

Day 5: Osaka for dinner

Thirty minutes by train and a completely different register: louder, later, and food-obsessed. Go in the evening and come back the same night.

Day 6: Ohara or Kurama

An hour north into the hills. Terraced temple gardens, almost no visitors, and an onsen at Kurama to finish the day.
