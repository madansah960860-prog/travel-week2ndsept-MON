From a rough idea to a plan you would actually follow

Five steps, about twenty minutes, and no account required until you want to save. Here is exactly what happens at each stage and what the software is doing behind it.

Try the planner See what Pro adds

Works on a phone, on a laptop, and on paper.

Step one: tell us what you already have

Not your destination. Your constraints. Nearly every planning mistake we see comes from choosing a destination first and discovering the constraints later.

The four inputs that matter

  • Vacation days available. Including or excluding the travel days at each end, your choice. The default assumes travel days count.
  • Rough total budget in USD. A range is fine. We use it to flag plans that drift past it, not to block you.
  • Departure airport. A trip from Newark and a trip from Boise are different trips, even to the same place.
  • Party size and mobility. Two adults, a family with a stroller, and a group of six move at genuinely different speeds.

Why the departure airport changes the plan

A nonstop from a large hub can make a five-day international trip reasonable. Two connections from a regional airport usually cannot, because you lose most of two days to travel.

Wildroot uses your departure airport to set the first and last day of the plan realistically: arrival-day energy is low, and departure day is mostly logistics. Those days get lighter schedules automatically.

If you hold TSA PreCheck or Global Entry, tell us. Airport buffers shrink by roughly 30 minutes domestically and 45 minutes on international return.

Step two: choose a starting point, or start blank

You can begin from one of our researched destinations, from a sample itinerary, or from an empty three-day board.

Every destination in our library ships with a stop library: the places we would put on a first visit, each with an opening time, a realistic visit duration, an entry cost in USD, and a note about when it gets crowded. You are not obliged to use any of them.

Typing your own stop is a first-class action, not a fallback. Give it a name and a duration and it behaves exactly like a library stop.

Step three: build the days and watch the pacing

This is the part other planners skip. A list of nine things you want to see is not an itinerary. An itinerary knows how long it takes to get between them.

Travel time between every stop

Shown as a chip under each stop: driving, walking or transit, with distance in miles. Times assume normal conditions, not empty roads.

Overpacked day warnings

If your stops plus travel exceed about eleven active hours, the day is flagged. You can override it. Most people move one stop instead.

Opening hours sanity check

A stop scheduled before it opens or after it closes is highlighted. Seasonal closures for library stops are noted on the card.

The pacing rule we use

Two anchor stops per day, plus one flexible stop, plus one meal you have actually thought about. Beyond that, quality drops fast. Our most-followed itineraries are the ones with the fewest items per day, which surprised us too when we looked at the data.

Step four: the budget adds itself up

Every stop carries an estimated cost per person. As you build, Wildroot totals them by day and for the whole trip, multiplied by your party size.

Costs are estimates, and we label them as estimates. Entry fees change, restaurants change, and exchange rates move. What the number is good for is comparison: seeing that swapping one guided tour for a self-guided walk saves more than skipping three lunches.

What the totals include and exclude

  • Included: activity and entry costs, tours you have added, meals you have added as stops, and local transit legs.
  • Included on Pro: a separate lodging line and a fuel estimate for driving routes.
  • Excluded: international flights, travel insurance, souvenirs, and tips.
  • Excluded: anything you have not added to a day. The planner does not guess at spending you have not planned.

A worked example

Three days in Kyoto for two travelers, built from our stop library:

Estimated activity spend, USD
Day 1$118
Day 2$164
Day 3$97
Three-day total$379

Lodging, flights and insurance sit outside this figure. Full breakdowns live on each destination page.

Step five: take it somewhere with no signal

A plan that lives only in a browser tab is a plan you will lose in a parking lot in Vik with no service.

  • Print directly from any browser. The print stylesheet strips the navigation, ads and footer and keeps the day cards.
  • Pro subscribers can export an offline PDF sized for a phone screen, with each day on its own page.
  • Pro subscribers can share one live link with everyone on the trip. Changes appear for the whole group.
