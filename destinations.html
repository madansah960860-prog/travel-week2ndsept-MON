Twelve more destinations, with the numbers up front

Each one has a researched stop library inside the trip planner. Daily budgets are per person and cover activities, local transport and food at a mid-range level. Flights and lodging sit outside the figure.

  • Portugal
  • 4 to 6 days
  • $115 per day

Lisbon

The destination we recommend most often for a first international trip from the US. English is widely spoken, the food is excellent at every price point, and a week costs roughly what a long weekend in a major US city does.

Best time: March to June, or late September into October. July and August are hot and packed. Getting around: metro, trams and your legs. Do not rent a car.

Signature stops: Alfama on foot, the Time Out Market, sunset from Senhora do Monte, a day trip to Sintra.

  • California, USA
  • 3 to 5 days
  • $230 per day

Big Sur and Highway 1

Ninety miles of coast that takes three days to drive properly. Lodging is the expensive part and books out months ahead; the driving itself is nearly free.

Best time: September and October, when the summer fog lifts. Watch for: road closures after winter storms. Check the highway status before you commit to a route.

Signature stops: Bixby Bridge, McWay Falls, Pfeiffer Beach at low tide, Point Lobos.

  • Central America
  • 7 to 10 days
  • $140 per day

Costa Rica

Short flights from most US hubs, and a country small enough on a map to fool people. The drive from Monteverde to Manuel Antonio looks like two hours and takes four and a half.

Best time: December to April is dry season. May and November are cheaper and wetter. Getting around: rent a 4x4 or use shuttle services between towns.

Signature stops: Monteverde cloud forest, Arenal hot springs, Manuel Antonio, Tortuguero canals.

  • Alberta, Canada
  • 5 to 8 days
  • $180 per day

Banff and Jasper

The easiest big-mountain trip from the US. Fly into Calgary, drive 90 minutes, and you are in the park. A passport is required, and the Icefields Parkway between the two towns is the point of the trip.

Best time: late June through September. Shoulder season means closed trails and snow on passes. Note: a Parks Canada pass is required and sold at the gate.

Signature stops: Moraine Lake shuttle, Lake Louise at sunrise, Peyto Lake, Athabasca Glacier.

  • Scotland
  • 6 to 9 days
  • $165 per day

Scottish Highlands

A driving trip with single-track roads, passing places and sheep that do not move. Americans consistently underestimate how long 60 miles takes here. Budget double what a US highway would need.

Best time: May, June and September. July and August bring midges and full car parks. Getting around: automatic rental cars cost more and sell out; book early.

Signature stops: Glencoe, Isle of Skye, Loch Ness southern shore, Cairngorms.

  • Louisiana, USA
  • 3 to 4 days
  • $175 per day

New Orleans

A long weekend that works from almost any US airport. Walkable, food-driven, and much better outside the French Quarter than in it. Book restaurants two to three weeks ahead.

Best time: February through May, and October through early December. Summer is humid enough to change your plans. Getting around: streetcars and walking.

Signature stops: the Garden District on foot, Frenchmen Street, a swamp tour, City Park.

  • Hawaii, USA
  • 6 to 9 days
  • $260 per day

Maui

Domestic, so no passport, but a long flight from the East Coast and a real time-zone adjustment. Plan the first day as a rest day. Travel respectfully and follow local guidance about which areas welcome visitors.

Best time: April to May and September to early November. Getting around: a rental car is essential and should be reserved months in advance.

Signature stops: Haleakala sunrise with a reservation, the Road to Hana done slowly, Iao Valley.

  • Mexico
  • 4 to 6 days
  • $105 per day

Mexico City

Short flights, excellent value, and one of the best food cities anywhere. It sits at 7,350 feet, so the first day should be gentle if you live near sea level.

Best time: March to May, and October to November. Getting around: the metro is cheap and fast; use registered ride apps at night.

Signature stops: Museo Nacional de Antropología, Coyoacán, Teotihuacán as a half day, a market food walk.

  • Central Europe
  • 5 to 7 days
  • $130 per day

Slovenia

Alps, caves and a short coastline in a country you can cross in three hours. It pairs well with a few days in Venice or Vienna if you are already flying to Europe.

Best time: late May to June, and September. Getting around: rent a car. Buses connect the main towns but not the trailheads.

Signature stops: Lake Bled early morning, Vintgar Gorge, Postojna Cave, Ljubljana old town.

  • Norway
  • 7 to 10 days
  • $245 per day

Norwegian Fjords

Expensive in the same way Iceland is expensive, and worth planning around ferries rather than roads. Alcohol and restaurant meals are where US travelers get caught out.

Best time: June through August for long daylight; September for fewer people. Getting around: trains, ferries and one rental car segment.

Signature stops: Bergen, the Flam railway, Geirangerfjord, Trolltunga if you are fit and prepared.

  • South Carolina, USA
  • 3 to 4 days
  • $190 per day

Charleston

A walkable long weekend with serious food and serious history. Choose tour operators that engage honestly with the city's role in slavery; the good ones are clearly better.

Best time: March to May, and October to November. Getting around: on foot downtown, with a car only for the beaches and plantations.

Signature stops: the historic district on foot, McLeod Plantation, Sullivan's Island, a harbor sunset.

  • Northern Italy
  • 6 to 8 days
  • $185 per day

The Dolomites

Mountain huts, cable cars and hiking that ranges from a stroll to genuinely technical. Fly into Venice or Innsbruck, then drive. Parking at trailheads fills before 8 AM in summer.

Best time: late June to mid September, once the passes clear. Getting around: a rental car, plus lift passes in the valleys you base in.

Signature stops: Tre Cime loop, Lago di Braies at dawn, Alpe di Siusi, Seceda ridgeline.
