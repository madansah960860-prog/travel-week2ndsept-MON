Affiliate Disclosure

Last updated 08/28/2026.

The short version. Some links on this site are affiliate links. If you book through one, the booking company may pay us a commission. You pay exactly the same price. An affiliate relationship never determines what we recommend, and we mark affiliate content on the page where it appears rather than only here.

This page is a starting template and not legal advice. Have it reviewed by a licensed attorney before launch.

1. What an affiliate link is

An affiliate link carries a tracking code identifying Wildroot Trip Studio as the source of a visit. If you follow one and complete a booking, the company you booked with pays us a percentage of their commission or a fixed referral fee.

The cost to you is unchanged. Affiliate programs are paid out of the supplier's own marketing budget, not added to your price. If you would rather not use one, type the company's address into your browser directly. Nothing on this site is gated behind an affiliate link and nothing stops working if you avoid them.

2. Why we disclose this the way we do

The US Federal Trade Commission requires that a material connection between an endorser and a seller be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, close to the recommendation, not buried in a footer or a separate page.

So we do both. Every page containing affiliate links carries a visible disclosure box above or adjacent to the content, and this page explains the whole arrangement. If you can see a recommendation, you can see whether it earns us money without hunting for it.

4. Categories of affiliate relationship

  • Accommodation. Hotel and vacation rental booking platforms.
  • Flights. Flight search and metasearch platforms.
  • Rental cars. Car rental aggregators and suppliers.
  • Tours and activities. Experience marketplaces and individual operators.
  • Travel insurance. Independent insurance providers, on a referral basis.

We name the specific partner on the page where the link appears. We do not maintain a hidden list.

5. What is never an affiliate link

  • National Park Service entry passes, timed-entry reservations and federal permits.
  • recreation.gov campground bookings.
  • Government sources, including the US Department of State and passport services.
  • Meteorological services, road authorities and emergency information.
  • Any link inside a safety note.

These are public resources. Putting a commission on them would be indefensible and we do not do it.

6. Our editorial rules

These are the constraints we hold ourselves to, and they are the reason the disclosure is worth reading rather than a formality.

  • Recommendations are made first, monetization second. Our researchers choose the stops and the routes. Only afterwards does anyone check whether a booking link exists.
  • Commission rate never affects ranking. We do not order destinations, stops or lodging areas by what pays best.
  • We recommend free alternatives when they are better. Several of our guides tell you to skip a paid tour, use a cheaper geothermal pool, or walk instead of taking a paid transfer. Those recommendations cost us money and they stay.
  • No partner previews our content. No hotel, airline, operator or platform sees a guide before publication or has any right of review.
  • No paid placement. We do not accept payment to appear in the destination library, to be added to a stop library, or to be named in an itinerary.
  • Critical coverage is not removed. If a partner's product is not good, we say so or we leave it out.

7. How much of our revenue this is

Affiliate commissions are our second-largest revenue source, behind Pro subscriptions and ahead of the custom itinerary service. We deliberately keep subscriptions as the majority of revenue, because a business that depends on commission has an incentive to push bookings rather than to give good advice.

The full picture of how we make money, in order of size, is on the About page and summarized on How It Works.

8. Advertising is separate

We do not currently run third-party display advertising on this site. If we introduce it, it will appear only on content pages in clearly labeled slots, never on legal pages, on the error page, or inside the trip planner. Advertising is not an affiliate relationship: we would not select those ads and would not be endorsing them. How advertising cookies work, and how to opt out of personalized ads, is covered in our Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy.

9. Questions or a complaint

If you think a recommendation on this site looks commercially motivated, tell us and we will explain the reasoning behind it or change it. Email hello@wildroottripstudio.com.
